Leads go missing when they live in personal WhatsApp chats and spreadsheets, because nobody can see which inquiries were answered, who owns them, or where each one stands. Connecting WhatsApp to a CRM fixes this: every message is logged against a contact, routine questions get an instant automated reply, and managers can see the whole pipeline in one place.

## Why does WhatsApp lose leads?

WhatsApp is where customers in Pakistan prefer to talk, so it is where most inquiries arrive. But a phone is not a sales system:

- Chats are spread across several staff phones, and leave with the person when they leave.
- There is no shared record of who replied, what was promised, or what happens next.
- Busy periods and evenings mean slow first replies, and slow replies lose leads.
- Copying details into a spreadsheet by hand is skipped when the team is busy.

## What changes with a CRM connected to WhatsApp?

- **Every inquiry becomes a record.** Each new chat creates or updates a contact in the CRM, with the full conversation attached.
- **Instant first replies.** An automated WhatsApp assistant answers common questions at any hour and collects the details your team needs.
- **Clear ownership.** Each lead is assigned to a person, with a status and a next step.
- **Visible performance.** Managers see how many leads came in, how fast they were answered, and how each team member is converting.

## Do you need a custom CRM or an off-the-shelf one?

An off-the-shelf CRM can work if your sales process is simple and standard. A custom CRM is worth it when your process has its own stages (for example applications, documents and visas for a consultancy), when you need WhatsApp at the centre of it, or when you want reports shaped around how your team actually works. For education consultancies, Webfluence also offers a ready-made [Education Consultancy CRM](/products/education-crm).

## How to start

1. List where leads arrive today (WhatsApp numbers, website forms, calls, walk-ins).
2. Write down the stages a lead moves through until it becomes a customer.
3. Note the questions customers ask most; these become the automated replies.
