A workable monthly social media plan comes down to four things: a small set of recurring post types, a fixed posting schedule you can actually keep, content produced in one batch at the start of the month, and a quick review of what worked at the end of it. Consistency matters more than volume.

## Step 1: Pick four or five post types

Recurring formats make planning faster and make your feed recognisable. For most businesses these cover it:

- **Product or service posts:** what you sell, with price or a clear next step.
- **Proof:** client results, testimonials, before-and-after, finished projects.
- **Behind the scenes:** your team, process or premises.
- **Helpful posts:** tips and answers to questions customers ask you.
- **Offers and news:** launches, seasonal offers, announcements.

## Step 2: Set a schedule you can keep

Choose a realistic number of posts a week, and the same days each week. Three good posts a week, every week, beats ten one week and none the next. Add short videos (Reels) where you can; they usually reach more people than static images.

## Step 3: Produce the month in one batch

Plan all the posts in one sitting at the start of the month, then shoot photos and video in one or two sessions. Write the captions together so the tone stays the same, and schedule everything in Meta Business Suite. Batching is what keeps social media from eating into every working day.

## Step 4: Keep the look consistent

Use the same fonts, colours and logo placement on every post. A consistent look is what makes a business appear professional. One client, AHM Developers, put it this way after Webfluence took over their social media: "Our social media finally looks consistent and professional."

## Step 5: Review at the end of the month

Look at which posts got the most saves, shares, messages and profile visits, not just likes. Do more of the formats that brought inquiries, and drop the ones that did not.

## Should you do it in-house or hand it over?

If someone on your team has the time and an eye for design, a plan like this is very manageable in-house.
