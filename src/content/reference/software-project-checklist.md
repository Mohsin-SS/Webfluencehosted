Preparing these answers before you talk to a software provider makes scoping faster and estimates more accurate. You do not need every answer. Gaps are normal and are worked out together.

## The problem

- What is not working today, in one or two sentences?
- What does it cost you: time, lost leads, errors, missed sales?
- How will you know the new system is working? Pick one or two measurable outcomes.

## The people

- Who will use the system: roles and roughly how many people?
- Who decides on requirements and signs off the work?
- Will customers, suppliers or agents use it, or only your staff?

## The process

- List the steps of the process the software must support, in order. For a CRM, that is how an inquiry becomes a customer; for an ERP, how stock moves or how an order is fulfilled.
- Note the exceptions: returns, cancellations, approvals, special cases.
- Collect the forms, spreadsheets and reports you use today. They show the data the system needs.

## The data

- What data must move into the new system (customers, products, stock, history)?
- Where does it live now, and how clean is it?

## Connections

- Which tools must it connect to? For example WhatsApp, your website or Shopify store, accounting software, payment gateways, SMS or email.

## Devices and access

- Desktop, mobile, or both? Is a mobile app needed, or will a mobile-friendly web app do?
- Do people need to work offline or from several locations?

## Priorities

- Which features are essential for the first version, and which can come later?
- Is there a deadline, such as a season, launch or audit?

## Budget and support

- Is there a budget range? Even a rough one helps shape the first version.
- After launch, who will look after the system: your team, the provider, or both?
