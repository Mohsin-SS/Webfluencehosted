Performance marketing glossary

Plain-English definitions of the terms you will see in Google Ads, Meta Ads and Google Analytics reports, and in Webfluence’s performance marketing reports.

Results and money

Conversion. An action you have told an ad platform to count as a success, such as a purchase, a form submission or a WhatsApp click. What you choose to count decides what the platform optimises for.

Key event. Google Analytics 4’s name for an event you have marked as important, such as purchase or generate_lead. Key events can be imported into Google Ads as conversions.

ROAS (return on ad spend). Revenue from ads divided by what the ads cost. Spending Rs 100,000 to make Rs 400,000 in sales is a ROAS of 4 (often written 4x or 400%).

CPA (cost per acquisition). What you pay in ad spend, on average, for one conversion: total spend divided by the number of conversions.

CPL (cost per lead). CPA where the conversion is a lead, such as an inquiry or form submission.

AOV (average order value). Total sales divided by the number of orders. Raising AOV grows revenue without needing more customers.

Conversion rate. The share of visitors or clicks that convert. 30 orders from 1,000 visits is a 3% conversion rate.

Ads and reach

Impressions. How many times an ad was shown. One person can produce several impressions.

Reach. How many different people saw an ad.

Frequency. Impressions divided by reach: how many times, on average, each person saw it. Rising frequency with falling results usually means the creative needs refreshing.

CTR (click-through rate). Clicks divided by impressions. A quick signal of whether an ad is relevant to the people seeing it.

CPC (cost per click). Ad spend divided by clicks.

Retargeting (remarketing). Showing ads to people who have already visited your website, viewed a product or engaged with your page.

Tracking

Pixel / tag. A small piece of code on your website that tells Meta (the Meta pixel) or Google (the Google tag) what visitors do, so ads can be measured and optimised.

Conversions API (CAPI). Meta’s server-to-server way of reporting events such as purchases, which is more reliable than the browser pixel alone.

UTM parameters. Tags added to a link (for example ?utm_source=instagram) so analytics can tell which campaign or post sent a visitor.

Attribution. The rules for deciding which ad or channel gets credit for a sale when a customer interacted with several before buying.

Search

SEO (search engine optimisation). Improving a website so it ranks higher in unpaid search results.

Technical SEO. The parts of SEO about how a site is built: speed, mobile-friendliness, crawlability, structured data and indexing.

Search Console. Google’s free tool that shows which searches your site appears for, and any indexing problems.

Schema markup (structured data). Code that describes a page’s content (a business, a service, an FAQ) in a format search engines and AI assistants can read reliably.
